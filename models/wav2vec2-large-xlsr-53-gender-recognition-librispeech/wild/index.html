Wild — the pitch-shift explorer

Take one voice and slide its playback rate from deep-and-slow to chipmunk-fast. If the checkpoint's label moves with the pitch, you've just watched the whole truth about this kind of model: it tracks acoustic properties — pitch and timbre — not a person. Every point on the chart is a real inference run in your browser.

Why this demo exists

“female” / “male” are binary acoustic voice labels from LibriSpeech training data. This page is the evidence: the same reader, same words, same recording — only the playback rate changed — can move the label. A model whose answer slides with a pitch knob is describing sound, and must never be used to infer a person's gender or identity, or for employment, access, safety, or any other consequential decision about someone.

Run it

Pick a sample voice, then run the sweep: the page resynthesizes the clip at seven playback rates (0.70×–1.40× — honest rate resynthesis: pitch and tempo shift together, no formant-preserving trickery) and classifies each variant for real. First use downloads ~319 MB; the sweep then runs seven inferences (well under a minute on desktop WASM).

What you're looking at

  • Rate resynthesis is honest but crude — playing audio faster raises pitch and shortens the clip together (the classic tape-speed effect). A production pitch shifter would preserve tempo; this demo deliberately uses the simplest possible transform so the mechanism is transparent.
  • The label tracks pitch/timbre — lower the rate and the “male” logit typically grows; raise it and “female” does. The model learned the acoustic correlate its training labels carried.
  • Out-of-distribution behaviour is visible — these are Polish, Italian, Spanish and Mandarin clips, far from LibriSpeech English audiobooks. Watch confidence change with rate; that instability is the bias disclosure made tangible.
  • This is the strongest argument on the site — if a pitch knob can move the answer, the answer was never about identity.
