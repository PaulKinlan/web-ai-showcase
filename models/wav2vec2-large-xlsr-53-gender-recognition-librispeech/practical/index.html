Practical — recording-catalogue QA triage (human-in-the-loop)

You maintain a library of voice recordings and its metadata says who recorded what. This tool batch-runs the checkpoint over a clip set, compares each acoustic read against expectations you supply from your own records, and flags mismatches and low-margin clips — so a person reviews them. It never decides anything by itself.

Triage, not verdicts

The labels here are binary acoustic voice labels from LibriSpeech training data — how a clip sounds, not who the speaker is. A “mismatch” flag means “the audio doesn't sound like your metadata says, have a human look”: the metadata can be wrong, the recording can be mislabelled, the voice can be out-of-distribution for this model, or the model can simply be wrong. Never use this output to infer a person's gender or identity, and never for employment, access, safety, or any other consequential decision about a person.

Run it

Four bundled rights-safe audiobook clips act as the catalogue batch. Optionally set the acoustic label your records expect for each clip (default: skip — no comparison), set a review-margin bar, then run the batch. First use downloads ~319 MB; each clip then takes a few seconds on WASM.

How a team would use this

      
    
        
  • Metadata drift checks — after a migration or bulk import, spot-check that voice-talent fields still match the audio; mismatches go to a human queue with the clip attached.
    • 
        
  • Low-margin = automatic escalation — clips under the margin bar are flagged regardless of agreement, because a coin-flip acoustic read is never evidence.
    • 
        
  • Everything stays local — catalogue audio (often contractual or personal) never leaves the machine; the CSV is generated in the page.
    • 
        
  • Documented limits — the model was trained on English audiobook narration; non-English, noisy, or very short clips will produce weaker margins, which is exactly why the margin column exists.
