Basics — two labels, one clip

The whole capability in one interaction: play a voice into the checkpoint and read its two acoustic labels, their probabilities, and — most importantly — how close the call was. A large score gap and a near-tie look identical if only the top label is shown; the margin exposes that difference but is not calibrated confidence.

Framing (same as the main page)

“female” / “male” here are binary acoustic voice labels from LibriSpeech training data — a description of how a clip sounds to the model, never a statement about a person's gender or identity. The binary label set erases non-binary people and the training data skews by accent, age and recording conditions. Never use this output for identity, employment, access, safety, or any other consequential decision about a person.

Run it

Use the rights-reviewed LibriSpeech recording, upload audio, or record up to 10 s. Every source is deterministically cropped or zero-padded to exactly 5.000 s at 16 kHz mono, matching the model card. First use downloads ~319 MB.

The abstention threshold is your review bar: when the winning margin (|p₁ − p₂|) is below it, the page refuses to name a label and says “ask a human” instead. Set it to 100% to see the abstain state on any clip.

What to notice

  • Both labels always show. A binary classifier's answer is only meaningful next to the runner-up — 99.9% / 0.1% and 51% / 49% are very different statements.
  • The margin is only a score gap. It is useful for showing near-ties, but it is not calibrated confidence or evidence about a person.
  • Five seconds means five seconds. The worker reports exactly how many source samples were copied, cropped, or zero-padded.
  • The checkpoint always emits its two configured labels. That forced binary output is a model limitation, not a fact about the reader.
