Wild: stress-test the detector

The model card's “~95% accuracy” was measured on long GPT-2 samples — its home turf. This page is the opposite: a gauntlet of adversarial probes designed to find the edges. Short texts, another language, obfuscation, a human+AI splice, code, shouty caps, and polished modern-LLM-style prose. Run the gauntlet and watch a confident detector turn uncertain — the most honest demo on this site is the one where the model is allowed to be wrong.

What the gauntlet teaches

  • Length is everything. At four words the model has almost no statistical signal — short human asides can score “machine-written”. Never score snippets.
  • Distribution shift breaks it. It learned “GPT-2-1.5B vs English WebText (2019)”. French prose, source code, and obfuscated text are none of those things — the score there is noise dressed as a number.
  • Era matters. Polished, modern chat-model-style prose often reads human to this detector: today's models write smoother than the GPT-2 it knows. This is why “AI detectors” age.
  • Splicing muddies the signal. Half human + half machine lands in the grey zone — which is exactly where a careful product should defer to a person.

Every score on this page is computed live by the model in your browser; the annotations explain why each probe is hard, they don't predict the outcome. Re-run the gauntlet after looking at a few examples of your own on the main page — the pattern (long, English, GPT-2-flavoured = confident; anything else = noise) is remarkably consistent.
